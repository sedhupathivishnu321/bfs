# Limitations, failure cases and deployment constraints (read before citing any number)

1. **The task is persistence-dominated.** The channel changes slowly, so "no change" is a strong forecaster; absolute gains of the learned models over it are small (≈ 0.02 dB at +1 s, ≈ 0.07 dB at +2 s on Protocol A) and the improvement over the *best* learned baseline at +1 s is **not statistically supported** (bootstrap CI includes 0). The improvement is supported at +2 s on Protocol A.
2. **Only 5 sites** (14 recordings). Between-site variance (std across sites ≈ 0.34 dB at +1 s) is far larger than between-model differences, so rankings can change with more sites. Distance/range effects are confounded with environment (site-level).
3. **On a chronological split (Protocol B) the proposed model is not better than gradient boosting** (inconclusive CI) – only better than persistence.
4. **Uncertainty is NOT reliable out-of-distribution.** On unseen sites PCT's 90 % interval covers only ≈ 34 % of outcomes (PCT-E) – badly overconfident – whereas constant-σ baselines calibrated on a validation site cover ≈ 72–79 %. The heteroscedastic head must not be used for risk-aware decisions across sites without recalibration. (Within-site, ablation coverage ≈ 0.87–0.90.)
5. **Stale data:** if the newest ≥ 1 s of observations is missing, PCT-E degrades to ≈ 0.555 dB, *worse than persistence (0.539)*. Sensor dropout and additive noise barely affect it, which partly reflects that forecasts are dominated by near-persistence behaviour.
6. **Some components are not justified by the ablation:** removing cross-receiver context or attention pooling *improved* Protocol-B MAE (0.567 vs 0.592 dB); removing gating gave 0.580. These components remain in the frozen final model because ablations were run after the configuration was fixed and must not feed back into test-time selection; a simplified model is the obvious next iteration. Components with measurable benefit: depthwise-separable convs, heteroscedastic head (for MAE), multi-feature inputs, and the AR prior at +2 s.
7. **Selection hygiene.** Validation sites used for selection/shrinkage fitting are test sites of other folds (selection was a 4-candidate tie, effect ≈ 0). The scale-normalisation design was motivated by a pilot on one test fold. The ablation uses the Protocol-B test segment for analysis only.
8. **Missing real data ⇒ simulation (Part II).** No public dataset has optical SNR, PDR, energy, latency, hybrid-link, controller or multi-node data. The 35 figures that could not be drawn from measurements are therefore drawn from a **simulator** (acoustic gain dynamics real; link budgets, optical attenuation/plumes, power, packets, rewards, battery, 1 s feedback delay assumed). They are labelled `[SIMULATION]`, are NOT evidence about real systems, and the assumptions are listed in `SIMULATION_ASSUMPTIONS.md`. No BELLHOP/physics-simulator baseline was built; the original PIGT-DT scaffold's synthetic numbers are not used anywhere.
9. **Optical module is tiny** (540 points from one lab setup, repeated measurements, BER floor 1e-5): the GBM surrogate's MAE of 0.13 decades is interpolation within that setup, not a general optical channel model. Distance units are cm.
10. **Compute/deployment:** all timings are on a 4-vCPU cloud container (no GPU); batch-1 CPU latency of PCT is 1.6 ms – slower than the LSTM/Transformer here despite fewer parameters (many small ops); PCT-E costs 3×. No pruning/quantisation/distillation was applied (listed as future work); no energy measurements.
11. **Gain, not SNR.** Absolute SNR needs a noise estimate that the dataset does not include.
12. Training is CPU-non-deterministic at the 1e-3 level; three seeds were used, not more.

## Part II (simulation) – additional limitations and honest findings
13. **Simulator shaped to have something to forecast.** Turbidity plumes that propagate between neighbouring nodes and a 1 s feedback delay were added after a first test showed that (a) nothing could beat persistence and (b) a reactive controller was within 2 % of the oracle. A graph-temporal digital twin is therefore evaluated in a world where graph and temporal structure exist by construction.
14. **A simulator bug was found and fixed mid-study**: hybrid-mode latency used the minimum of acoustic/optical latency even when the optical packet failed, which made hybrid look artificially good. All controller/ablation/robustness/scalability results were regenerated after the fix (regression test in `tests/test_sim.py`). Earlier-reported controller rankings from before the fix are void.
15. **Digital-twin gains are small.** PIGT-DT's acoustic-SNR error is ≈3 % lower than the LSTM/GRU/Data-only baselines; on active optical links it is *not* better than TCN/LSTM or its own "no uncertainty" variant. Components: removing the graph costs ≈3.7 % on acoustic SNR; removing the physics prior only ≈0.3 %; removing the temporal module costs ≈30 %.
16. **Controller-level effects are tiny relative to the oracle gap**: PIGT-DT planner beats the reactive controller by +0.022 reward (CI [+0.003, +0.042]) and closes ≈ 24 % of the reactive→oracle gap; MAPPO+DT is nominally higher still (1.168 vs 1.140) but the difference is inconclusive. MAPPO without DT forecasts is *below* the reactive controller and even slightly below the tuned threshold heuristic (1.070 vs 1.081); the reactive, PIGT-DT and MAPPO+DT controllers beat the heuristic on reward, but the heuristic uses the least energy (0.705 vs ≈0.75–0.79 J/step) and has a violation rate (16.3 %) similar to the oracle's (16.2 %); MAPPO+DT has the lowest violation rate (13.2 %). Removing any single DT component, changing ensemble size (1–5), or changing the real/synthetic training mix moved controller reward by ≤ 0.013 – not distinguishable from noise. Using the DT's uncertainty (Monte-Carlo or risk-averse) *lowered* reward.
17. **The risk term behaves unexpectedly**: removing the reliability-violation penalty from the planner raised realised reward (1.188 vs 1.140) while raising the violation rate (17.7 % → 25.3 %): the indicator on a noisy forecast is a crude proxy and the cautious planner pays energy/latency for violations that often do not materialise.
18. **Staleness result**: with only 0.25 s feedback delay the plain reactive controller is *better* than the DT planner (1.171 vs 1.158); the DT helps only when the delay is ≥ 1 s.
19. **Out-of-distribution**: in `test_ood` (unseen turbidity/distances/noise) optical is infeasible everywhere, so all planners collapse to acoustic-only and tie with the oracle; MAPPO/MAPPO+DT/heuristic do *not* generalise (reward −1.75 vs −0.77) – they keep choosing actions learned in-distribution.
20. **DT uncertainty is conservative in simulation** (90 % intervals cover 94–97 %) because the simulated noise is exactly Gaussian; this says nothing about calibration on real links (contrast with the real-data PCT head in Part I, which was badly overconfident across sites).
21. **No GPU** was available: scalability reports CPU latency/memory/training time only. MAPPO training used 250 iterations × 3 seeds; longer training or tuning might change the RL ranking.
22. One-step planners and PPO only: no multi-step MPC, queueing, retransmission, MAC contention or inter-node interference.
