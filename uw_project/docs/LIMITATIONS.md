# Limitations, failure cases and deployment constraints (read before citing any number)

1. **The task is persistence-dominated.** The channel changes slowly, so "no change" is a strong forecaster; absolute gains of the learned models over it are small (≈ 0.02 dB at +1 s, ≈ 0.07 dB at +2 s on Protocol A) and the improvement over the *best* learned baseline at +1 s is **not statistically supported** (bootstrap CI includes 0). The improvement is supported at +2 s on Protocol A.
2. **Only 5 sites** (14 recordings). Between-site variance (std across sites ≈ 0.34 dB at +1 s) is far larger than between-model differences, so rankings can change with more sites. Distance/range effects are confounded with environment (site-level).
3. **On a chronological split (Protocol B) the proposed model is not better than gradient boosting** (inconclusive CI) – only better than persistence.
4. **Uncertainty is NOT reliable out-of-distribution.** On unseen sites PCT's 90 % interval covers only ≈ 34 % of outcomes (PCT-E) – badly overconfident – whereas constant-σ baselines calibrated on a validation site cover ≈ 72–79 %. The heteroscedastic head must not be used for risk-aware decisions across sites without recalibration. (Within-site, ablation coverage ≈ 0.87–0.90.)
5. **Stale data:** if the newest ≥ 1 s of observations is missing, PCT-E degrades to ≈ 0.555 dB, *worse than persistence (0.539)*. Sensor dropout and additive noise barely affect it, which partly reflects that forecasts are dominated by near-persistence behaviour.
6. **Some components are not justified by the ablation:** removing cross-receiver context or attention pooling *improved* Protocol-B MAE (0.567 vs 0.592 dB); removing gating gave 0.580. These components remain in the frozen final model because ablations were run after the configuration was fixed and must not feed back into test-time selection; a simplified model is the obvious next iteration. Components with measurable benefit: depthwise-separable convs, heteroscedastic head (for MAE), multi-feature inputs, and the AR prior at +2 s.
7. **Selection hygiene.** Validation sites used for selection/shrinkage fitting are test sites of other folds (selection was a 4-candidate tie, effect ≈ 0). The scale-normalisation design was motivated by a pilot on one test fold. The ablation uses the Protocol-B test segment for analysis only.
8. **Missing real data ⇒ missing experiments.** No optical SNR, PDR, throughput time series, energy, latency, controller, hybrid or multi-node link data are available, so 35 of the 70 requested figures were **not produced** (see `results/plots/INDEX.md`). No reinforcement-learning/MAPPO controller, digital-twin graph model or physics-only (BELLHOP) baseline was built or evaluated; the original PIGT-DT scaffold's numbers are synthetic and are not used anywhere.
9. **Optical module is tiny** (540 points from one lab setup, repeated measurements, BER floor 1e-5): the GBM surrogate's MAE of 0.13 decades is interpolation within that setup, not a general optical channel model. Distance units are cm.
10. **Compute/deployment:** all timings are on a 4-vCPU cloud container (no GPU); batch-1 CPU latency of PCT is 1.6 ms – slower than the LSTM/Transformer here despite fewer parameters (many small ops); PCT-E costs 3×. No pruning/quantisation/distillation was applied (listed as future work); no energy measurements.
11. **Gain, not SNR.** Absolute SNR needs a noise estimate that the dataset does not include.
12. Training is CPU-non-deterministic at the 1e-3 level; three seeds were used, not more.
